The Halbeisen Chronology

When each case takes place, from the text alone

Each date below is worked out from what the story itself says: stated dates, weekdays tested against the calendar, seasons, Watson’s marriage and lodgings, and references to other cases. No other chronologist was consulted. Cases are in the order we date them; 31 of 60 are done so far. Click a quoted passage to open the story at that place, with the passage marked.

A Study in Scarlet 4 March 1881 · medium confidence

The case begins on 4 March 1881: Watson gives the day and month but never the year, which follows from his nine months' sick leave after Maiwand and Jefferson Hope's 'twenty years' since 1860. Watson first met Holmes some weeks earlier, about January 1881 (no later than early February), and moved into Baker Street the next day. between 4 March 1881 and 4 March 1882

  • “It was upon the 4th of March, as I have good reason to remember” Day and month of the summons to Lauriston Gardens and of Holmes's revelation of his trade, the first morning of the Drebber case. No year is given anywhere in the story.
  • “with permission from a paternal government to spend the next nine months in attempting to improve it” Nine months' leave counted from landing at Portsmouth. Watson was wounded at Maiwand, then 'for months' ill with enteric fever, then a month at sea, so landing is late in 1880 at the earliest (Maiwand was fought on 27 July 1880: unverified, the text gives no date). The following 4 March is therefore March 1881; by March 1882 the leave would have run out.
  • “That girl that I spoke of was to have married me twenty years ago.” Hope's own reckoning. With the grave-board 'Died August 4th, 1860' (John Ferrier) the murders fall about 1880-81; 'twenty long years of longing' is a round figure, so 1881 is the nearest fit and 1879 or 1884 are far out.
  • “As the weeks went by, my interest in him and my curiosity as to his aims in life, gradually deepened and increased.” Watson had been lodging with Holmes for some weeks before 4 March, so the meeting at the hospital laboratory is well before it.
  • “During the first week or so we had no callers” After a first quiet week callers follow (Lestrade 'three or four times in a single week', a young girl, others) over a further stretch of time. The move to Baker Street is therefore at least three to five weeks before 4 March, i.e. no later than early February 1881.
  • “That very evening I moved my things round from the hotel” Watson inspected the rooms the day after Stamford introduced him to Holmes (Chapter II opens 'We met next day'), and moved in that evening. Landing in late 1880 and then living 'for some time' in a Strand hotel puts the meeting about January 1881.
  • “The Standard commented upon the fact that lawless outrages of the sort usually occurred under a Liberal Administration.” A Liberal government is in office; Gladstone's ministry ran from April 1880 to June 1885 (unverified), which agrees with 1881 and not with 1879.
  • “and this is the slack season” The landlady's remark on the lodgers' high rent fits March, an off-season month for London boarding-houses. Season only, no year.

Contradictions in the text

  • “The two bade adieu to their landlady upon Tuesday, the 4th inst., and departed to Euston Station” The Standard gives 'Tuesday, the 4th' for the departure from Camberwell. Watson dates the next morning 4 March and Lestrade puts the Euston sighting on 'the evening of the third'; 4 March was a Tuesday only in 1879, 1884 and 1890, years the Afghan war and the nine months' leave exclude (it was a Friday in 1881). One newspaper item against two other statements: the paper's weekday and date are the slip.
  • “They had been seen together at Euston Station about half-past eight on the evening of the third.” Lestrade's 'the third' (and the body found 'at two in the morning', so the 4th) disagrees with the Standard's 'Tuesday, the 4th inst.' for the departure; the evening of 3 March is taken as correct.
  • “In three years he was better off than his neighbours, in six he was well-to-do, in nine he was rich, and in twelve there were not half a dozen men” The Utah flashback counts twelve years from May 1847 to the June meeting with Lucy (1859), yet Ferrier's grave-board reads 'Died August 4th, 1860', a month after that meeting. The only exact date (1860) agrees with 'twenty years ago' and 1881; the 'twelve' is the loose figure.

The Speckled Band April 1883 · high confidence

Early April 1883, stated by Watson, while he still shared rooms with Holmes. Helen Stoner's account (sister died two years ago, engagement at Christmas two years ago, her own engagement a month before) is consistent. between 1 April 1883 and 10 April 1883

  • “It was early in April in the year ’83 that I woke one morning to find Sherlock Holmes standing, fully dressed, by the side of my bed” Explicit month and year.
  • “when we were sharing rooms as bachelors in Baker Street” Watson is unmarried and lodging at 221B, so before his marriage.
  • “Julia went there at Christmas two years ago” Counting back from April 1883 gives Christmas 1880; consistent.
  • “She died just two years ago, and it is of her death that I wish to speak to you.” Julia's death was about April 1881.
  • “but in a month or six weeks I shall be married” Wedding due in May or early June 1883.
  • “It is a little cold for the time of the year” Cold early-April morning.
  • “from which I have only been freed during the last month by the untimely death of the lady to whom the pledge was given” Written some years later, after Helen's death.

Contradictions in the text

  • “I shall walk down to Doctors’ Commons” Wills were kept at Somerset House by 1883 (unverified), so this is a minor anachronism; it does not affect the date.

The Yellow Face March 1887 · low confidence

Early spring (about March), with Watson living at Baker Street; the year is not stated. A voluntary transfer of a wife's property points to a marriage after 1882 (unverified), and Watson's marriage after September 1888 closes the window, so 1886 to spring 1889, with 1887 a weak favourite. between 1 March 1886 and 30 April 1889

  • “One day in early spring he had so far relaxed as to go for a walk with me in the Park” Season of day 1 (a Saturday by the counting of days).
  • “the sticky spear-heads of the chestnuts were just beginning to burst into their five-fold leaves” Bud-burst: late March to mid April.
  • “we could see in the light of the station lamps that he was very pale” Lamps needed on a 7 p.m. arrival: March or early April, consistent with early spring.
  • “I am a married man, and have been so for three years.” Munro's marriage is three years old, fixing nothing alone but see the next item.
  • “When we married, my wife made over all her property to me” A voluntary transfer 'rather against my will' suggests a marriage after the Married Women's Property Act took effect on 1 January 1883 (unverified), so the case is no earlier than early 1886.
  • “my friend and I have listened to a good many strange secrets in this room” A long partnership: well after the move in 1881.
  • “Save for the occasional use of cocaine, he had no vices” Cocaine only occasional: unlike the habitual injections 'three times a day for many months' of SIGN (September 1888) and the heavy period before SCAN (March 1888), so the spring of 1888 is the least likely of the candidates.

Contradictions in the text

  • “Well, about six weeks ago she came to me.” Munro says six weeks, then 'Nearly two months.'; two figures for the same time, a minor slip.

The Five Orange Pips September 1887 · high confidence

Late September 1887: Watson says so ('the latter days of September'; 'the year ’87') and Openshaw's own count (January 1885 plus two years and eight months) gives September 1887. The text's references to Watson's marriage and to the Sign of the Four sit badly with 1887. between 20 September 1887 and 30 September 1887

  • “It was in the latter days of September, and the equinoctial gales had set in with exceptional violence.” Time of year: after the equinox, late September.
  • “It was in January, ’85, that my poor father met his end, and two years and eight months have elapsed since then.” January 1885 plus 2 years 8 months is September 1887.
  • “The year ’87 furnished us with a long series of cases of greater or less interest” Watson places the case in 1887.
  • “One day—it was in March, 1883—a letter with a foreign stamp lay upon the table” Earlier family dates (10 March and 2 May 1883) are consistent with the 1885 and 1887 counts.
  • “My wife was on a visit to her mother’s, and for a few days I was a dweller once more in my old quarters at Baker Street.” Watson is already married in September 1887.
  • “Very long and very severe were the equinoctial gales that year.” Confirms the autumn gales of that year.

Contradictions in the text

  • “My wife was on a visit to her mother’s” Watson is married in September 1887 here, yet NOBL (October 1887) says his marriage is still weeks away, and SIGN, which this story cites, has dating pointing to 1888.
  • ““Save, perhaps, the Sign of Four.”” Treats SIGN as already past in September 1887; SIGN's own text (Dec 1878 'nearly ten years ago') points to about September 1888.
  • “I have been beaten four times—three times by men, and once by a woman.” If the woman is Irene Adler (SCAN, 20 March 1888) she is in the future in September 1887; the identification is a guess.
  • ““It is really two days since you had the letter.” Openshaw said the letter came 'yesterday morning', so one day, not two.

The Noble Bachelor 6 October 1887 · medium confidence

Early October 1887: Lord St Simon, born in 1846, is 'forty-one', and the hotel bill is dated 4 October with the wedding on the Wednesday (5 October 1887), so Holmes is consulted a day or two later. It is set 'a few weeks before my own marriage', which conflicts with SCAN, FIVE and the date of SIGN. between 5 October 1887 and 8 October 1887

  • “Born in 1846.’ He’s forty-one years of age” Year 1887 by age arithmetic.
  • “‘Oct. 4th, rooms 8s., breakfast 2s. 6d., cocktail 1s., lunch 2s. 6d., glass sherry, 8d.’” Hotel bill dated 4 October, within a week of the case.
  • “Two days later—that is, on Wednesday last—there is a curt announcement that the wedding had taken place” Wedding on a Wednesday; 5 October 1887 was a Wednesday.
  • “for the weather had taken a sudden turn to rain, with high autumnal winds” Autumn.
  • “It was a few weeks before my own marriage, during the days when I was still sharing rooms with Holmes in Baker Street” Set before Watson's marriage.
  • “how to while away these bleak autumnal evenings” Closing line confirms autumn.
  • “Frank here and I met in ’84, in McQuire’s camp, near the Rockies” Hatty's history fits a wedding about three years later, 1887.

Contradictions in the text

  • “It was a few weeks before my own marriage” Watson is married by March 1888 (SCAN) and already in September 1887 (FIVE); if his marriage follows SIGN (about September 1888) this story would be October 1888, but St Simon's age says 1887.
  • ““My last client of the sort was a king.”” Holmes's royal client in SCAN was the King of Bohemia (March 1888), called here 'the King of Scandinavia'; if the same, this story is after March 1888, contradicting 1887.

A Scandal in Bohemia 20 March 1888 · high confidence

The story opens on the evening of 20 March 1888, a date Watson states outright; the case runs over that evening and the next day. The text's own weekday arithmetic (betrothal on 'next Monday', 'three days yet') does not fit 20 March, which was a Tuesday. between 16 March 1888 and 23 March 1888

  • “One night—it was on the twentieth of March, 1888—I was returning from a journey to a patient” Explicit date of the first evening, and of the King's visit at a quarter to eight that night.
  • “I had seen little of Holmes lately. My marriage had drifted us away from each other.” Watson is already married and living apart, so the story is after his marriage.
  • “If you will be good enough to call to-morrow afternoon at three o’clock” The case runs over a second day, 21 March; the wedding and the departure of Adler follow on that day.
  • “It was already dusk, and the lamps were just being lighted” Dusk at about ten to seven fits late March (around the equinox); the fire and the ulster fit the cool season.
  • “which must always be associated in my mind with my wooing” The Sign of the Four, with Watson's wooing, is already past, so SIGN precedes March 1888 on this text.
  • “by binding you both to absolute secrecy for two years; at the end of that time the matter will be of no importance” Watson may publish only after two years, consistent with writing in or after 1890 (published 1891).

Contradictions in the text

  • “betrothal was publicly proclaimed. That will be next Monday.” A Monday follows 'three days yet' (next quote) only if the first evening is a Friday (16 or 23 March 1888). 20 March 1888 was a Tuesday, so the next Monday is six days away.
  • ““Oh, then we have three days yet,” said Holmes with a yawn.” Contradicts the stated 20 March (a Tuesday); one of the two statements is a slip.
  • “‘Come, man, come, only three minutes, or it won’t be legal.’” A noon deadline for a church wedding fits the law before 1886 (unverified), which sits oddly with 1888.
  • “which must always be associated in my mind with my wooing” SIGN's own text (Mary Morstan's father vanished 3 December 1878, 'nearly ten years ago'; a September evening) points to about September 1888, after this story; Watson's marriage cannot both precede March 1888 and follow SIGN.

A Case of Identity September 1888 · low confidence

After March 1888 (it cites the Irene Adler case) and with Holmes still alive, the only Fridays the 14th that fit are 14 Sept 1888, 14 Dec 1888, 14 June 1889, and 14 Feb, 14 Mar or 14 Nov 1890. No year is stated; September 1888 is the weak favourite on weather (straw hat with fur boa) and the 'Hague last year' link. between 14 September 1888 and 14 November 1890

  • “on the morning of the fourteenth, a gentleman named Hosmer Angel” The wedding morning was the 14th of a month.
  • “Your wedding was arranged, then, for the Friday.” The 14th was a Friday, which restricts the month and year.
  • “I advertised for him in last Saturday’s Chronicle” Ad on Saturday the 15th; the consultation falls in the following week (a few days after the 14th).
  • “It is a little souvenir from the King of Bohemia in return for my assistance in the case of the Irene Adler papers.” After SCAN (20 March 1888), so not before March 1888.
  • “I forgot that I had not seen you for some weeks.” Watson is living apart and married; supports the married period.
  • “there was something of the sort at The Hague last year” A Hague case 'last year'; possibly linked to the Dutch royal commission done before March 1888 (unverified link), which would favour 1888.
  • “a slate-coloured, broad-brimmed straw hat” A straw hat with a fur boa and muff suggests a spring or autumn day, not deep winter or high summer.

Contradictions in the text

  • “a large woman with a heavy fur boa round her neck” Fur boa, muff and a fire in the grate imply cold weather, while a straw hat implies mild weather; the season cannot be fixed.

The Sign of the Four September 1888 · medium confidence

September 1888: Watson says 'a September evening', and the year follows from Mary Morstan's dates (father vanished 3 December 1878, 'nearly ten years ago'; first pearl 4 May 1882, 'about six years ago'). The letter's postmark 'July 7' is the odd one out against the fog, the grouse and the evening twilight. The story ends with Watson accepted by Miss Morstan, so his marriage is after September 1888. between 7 July 1888 and 30 September 1888

  • “He disappeared upon the 3rd of December, 1878,” Mary's fixed date, followed by 'nearly ten years ago': between early 1888 and 3 December 1888, so the case is 1888.
  • “to be exact, upon the 4th of May, 1882” 'About six years ago' (late 1887 to late 1888). Holmes later says 'they were six years looking for it', and Mary 'must be seven-and-twenty now' (seventeen in 1878): all give 1888.
  • “It was a September evening, and not yet seven o’clock” Watson's own statement of the month for the first evening (the cab to Thaddeus Sholto and the night at Pondicherry Lodge).
  • “It was twilight before we reached the Tower.” The launch left Westminster 'a little past seven' on the chase evening a few days later; twilight by about half past seven means sunset between about six and seven o'clock, i.e. early to mid September (London sunset about 6.15-6.30 pm GMT; on 7 July about 8.10 pm, still broad daylight).
  • “It is very hot for the time of year” Athelney Jones remarks on the heat as unusual for the season: not midsummer, and fitting a warm spell in early autumn rather than July.
  • “I have oysters and a brace of grouse” Holmes's dinner on the chase evening; grouse are in season from 12 August (unverified), so it is after mid-August and cannot be 7 July.
  • “Miss Morstan has done me the honour to accept me as a husband in prospective.” Watson is engaged on the last night of the case (September 1888). He is not yet married, so his marriage follows the case; SIGN gives no interval.
  • “I even embodied it in a small brochure with the somewhat fantastic title of ‘A Study in Scarlet.’” SIGN follows STUD: Watson has already written up the Jefferson Hope case.
  • “More than once during the years that I had lived with him in Baker Street” Watson has shared Baker Street for years and still does; about seven and a half years since January 1881 fits September 1888.

Contradictions in the text

  • “Postmark, London, S.W. Date, July 7.” The letter 'received this morning' on the first day is postmarked 7 July, yet Chapter III, the same day, opens 'It was a September evening'. One postmark against the narrator's month, the fog and drizzle, the grouse, the twilight and Jones's 'time of year': the postmark is taken as the slip. Also 7 July 1888 was a Saturday, whereas the advertisement ('last Tuesday morning') implies the murder night was a Monday.
  • “showed me six of the finest pearls that I had ever seen.” One pearl comes every 4 May from 1882 (1882 to 1887 is six; 1888 would be a seventh), so six pearls puts the case before 4 May 1888. That disagrees with both July and September 1888 and with 'about six years' and 'nearly ten years'; the pearl count is the slip (or the 1888 pearl is missing).
  • “At last, however, some three or four years ago, we found ourselves in England.” Small puts his arrival in England three or four years before 1888 (c. 1884-85), but he watched at Major Sholto's window when the Major died on 28 April 1882, six years earlier, and 'six years looking for it' agrees with 1882. 'Three or four' is the slip.
  • “He’s been away since yesterday mornin’, sir” Mrs Smith says on the morning after the murder night that her husband left 'yesterday mornin'', yet she also says the wooden-legged man woke him 'about three' the night before, and the advertisement says 'at or about three o’clock last Tuesday morning'; these are one set of early hours, so 'yesterday' is loose. Holmes's 'eight-and-twenty hours’ start' likewise does not fit a murder at about ten the evening before and a start at dawn.

Order against other cases

  • “But you have yourself had some experience of my methods of work in the Jefferson Hope case.” after STUD: Holmes and Watson look back on the Jefferson Hope case, already written up as 'A Study in Scarlet', so SIGN follows STUD (March 1881).

Silver Blaze October 1888 · low confidence

Autumn (September or October): a Thursday, the crime on the Monday night before, and a race the following Tuesday. The year is not stated; Watson is living at Baker Street and the 'memoirs' already exist, so it falls after STUD was written up and, if he is unmarried, before the winter of 1888-89. between 1 September 1887 and 31 October 1890

  • “the low curves of the moor, bronze-coloured from the fading ferns, stretched away to the sky-line” Bracken turning: autumn, roughly late September or October.
  • “deepening into rich, ruddy browns where the faded ferns and brambles caught the evening light” Second seasonal marker, again autumn.
  • “He has twice lodged at Tavistock in the summer.” Summer is spoken of as past, so the case is after summer.
  • “the stables were locked up at nine o’clock” 'Very dark' soon after nine on the Monday night also points away from midsummer.
  • “a more common occurrence than any one would think who only knew me through your memoirs” Watson's published accounts of Holmes already exist, so the case is after STUD was written up (the SIGN text shows it written by September 1888).
  • “Silver Blaze,” said he, “is from the Isonomy stock, and holds as brilliant a record as his famous ancestor.” If the real stallion Isonomy is meant (at stud from 1881: unverified), a four-year-old descendant cannot be earlier than 1887.
  • ““Tuesday evening!” I exclaimed. “And this is Thursday morning.” Opening day is a Thursday, the crime Monday night, the race 'next Tuesday'. These weekday counts do not fix the year.

Contradictions in the text

  • “Four days later Holmes and I were again in the train” The race was twice said to be on Tuesday, five days after the Thursday visit; 'Four days later' from the Thursday (or Friday) is Monday (Tuesday only if counted from the Friday return to London). A slip of one day.

Order against other cases

  • “a more common occurrence than any one would think who only knew me through your memoirs” after STUD: Holmes speaks as if Watson's accounts have been published, so after the writing-up of STUD.

The Beryl Coronet February 1889 · low confidence

A February with deep snow; the year is not stated. Watson is living at Baker Street and unmarried, so it is a February between STUD (1881) and his marriage, which SIGN puts after September 1888; February 1889 is the only such winter after SIGN and is a weak favourite. between 1 February 1882 and 28 February 1889

  • “It was a bright, crisp February morning, and the snow of the day before still lay deep upon the ground” Month and weather of day 2 of the case (a Friday by the counting below): February, hard frost and deep snow. No year.
  • “said I as I stood one morning in our bow-window looking down the street” Watson is a resident at 221B with no wife or practice mentioned: a bachelor-period case, i.e. after the move in January 1881 (STUD) and before his marriage. SIGN ends with his engagement in September 1888, so he is not married before then.
  • “Next Monday I have a large sum due to me” The loan is made 'yesterday morning' (day 1) for repayment 'next Monday'; with 'in four days' this makes day 1 a Thursday, Holmes's visit Friday and the second interview Saturday. It does not fix the year: every February has such days.
  • “I should be able in four days to reclaim it.” Confirms the Thursday-to-Monday count; no year.
  • “It is an old maxim of mine that when you have excluded the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth.” Holmes calls it an 'old maxim'; its first statement in the canon (SIGN, 'eliminated the impossible') is in September 1888. A weak lean to after SIGN, not proof (unverified that it was first stated there).
  • “Your skill has indeed exceeded all that I have heard of it.” Holmes's name is already known in banking and society circles. In SIGN (September 1888) Holmes still says 'My name figures in no newspaper', so a lean to later rather than earlier.

The Stockbroker's Clerk June 1889 · medium confidence

A Saturday in June, three months after Watson took over a Paddington practice bought 'shortly after my marriage'; Holmes asks after Mrs Watson's recovery from 'our adventure of the Sign of Four', so it follows SIGN (September 1888) and the first June after is June 1889. It implies Watson married between October 1888 and about March 1889. between 1 June 1889 and 29 June 1889

  • “Shortly after my marriage I had bought a connection in the Paddington district.” Watson is married and has left Baker Street; the purchase of the practice follows the marriage closely.
  • “For three months after taking over the practice I was kept very closely at work” The case is about three months after Watson took over the practice (so the practice was bought about March, and the marriage a little before).
  • “I trust that Mrs. Watson has entirely recovered from all the little excitements connected with our adventure of the Sign of Four.” Holmes speaks as if SIGN is fairly recent; the SIGN case is September 1888, so the earliest June is 1889.
  • “one morning in June, as I sat reading the British Medical Journal after breakfast” Month of the case; the day is a Saturday: June 1889 Saturdays are the 1st, 8th, 15th, 22nd and 29th.
  • “They shut at twelve on Saturdays.” Mawson's offices close at midday on Saturday, and Pycroft says he worked 'until Friday, that is, yesterday': the case day is a Saturday.
  • “which a man would hardly do even in so wet a June as this” A cold wet June with fires still lit.
  • “they were let in early in the spring through the Venezuelan loan” Pycroft's firm failed 'early in the spring' of the same year and he has been out of work some months; no real event is named (unverified).

Order against other cases

  • “I trust that Mrs. Watson has entirely recovered from all the little excitements connected with our adventure of the Sign of Four.” after SIGN: The Sign of Four adventure, which Mrs Watson shared, is past, and recent enough that Holmes asks after her recovery.

The Man with the Twisted Lip June 1889 · medium confidence

Watson dates it June 1889, and St Clair's history (settled at Lee May 1884, married 1887, two children) fits 1889. The stated 'Friday, June 19th' fits 1891 and 1885 instead (19 June 1889 was a Wednesday), a conflict recorded here. between 14 June 1889 and 28 June 1889

  • “One night—it was in June, ’89—there came a ring to my bell” Explicit month and year.
  • ““Of Friday, June 19th.”” Gives weekday and date; the Friday is probably Friday 21 June 1889 if the weekday is right.
  • “Some years ago—to be definite, in May, 1884—there came to Lee a gentleman, Neville St. Clair by name” Settled in Lee in May 1884; narrative is some years later.
  • “in 1887 he married the daughter of a local brewer, by whom he now has two children” After 1887 and plausibly two or more years after (1889), ruling out 1885.
  • “Your wife has been waiting this two days for you.” Whitney lost two days from Wednesday, so Watson's night in the den is a Friday.
  • “If you remember, Monday was an exceedingly hot day” Hot June weather; the disappearance was the Monday of the same week.
  • “It was twenty-five minutes past four.” Bright summer dawn light at 4.25 a.m. fits June.

Contradictions in the text

  • ““Of Friday, June 19th.”” 19 June 1889 was a Wednesday (Friday was 21 June). 'Friday, June 19th' matches 1891 and 1885 exactly. 1885 is ruled out by the 1887 marriage; 1891 is ruled out by Holmes's absence after the Reichenbach events of May 1891 (FINA) and by Watson's own 'June, ’89'.
  • ““Good heavens! I thought it was Wednesday. It is Wednesday.” Whitney insists it is Wednesday, which would match 19 June 1889; Watson overrules, so the story's own weekday is Friday.

The Boscombe Valley Mystery 6 June 1889 · high confidence

The murder was on Monday 3 June, which is a Monday in 1889 (not 1888 or 1890); with Watson married and the text's later reference to SIGN, the case is 1889. Holmes reads the report after the Wednesday hearing, so he sets out about Thursday 6 June 1889. between 6 June 1889 and 8 June 1889

  • “On June 3rd, that is, on Monday last” Murder date; 3 June fell on a Monday in 1889 (also 1878 and 1895).
  • “he was on Wednesday brought before the magistrates at Ross” Inquest Tuesday 4th, magistrates Wednesday 5th, so Holmes is reading on Thursday 6th or soon after.
  • “We were seated at breakfast one morning, my wife and I” Watson is married, which rules out 1878 and fits after SIGN.
  • “seeing what I gained through one of them” Refers to his wife won through a Holmes case (SIGN), so after SIGN.
  • “had only just returned upon the morning of last Monday, the 3rd” Second statement that the 3rd was the Monday.
  • “There was no rain, as Holmes had foretold, and the morning broke bright and cloudless.” Settled fine weather, as in early June.
  • “Old Turner lived for seven months after our interview, but he is now dead” Writing after about January 1890; consistent with publication in 1891 (and excludes 1895).

The Engineer's Thumb July 1889 · medium confidence

Summer 1889, as Watson states, soon after his marriage and while he has his own practice near Paddington. The text gives no month; the moon, dawn and rose-bushes only confirm summer. between 1 June 1889 and 22 September 1889

  • “It was in the summer of ’89, not long after my marriage, that the events occurred which I am now about to summarise” Explicit season and year.
  • “the lapse of two years has hardly served to weaken the effect” Written about two years later (1891), consistent with publication.
  • “for the moon had sunk, and a bright morning was breaking when I came to myself” Dawn light and a moon up after midnight; consistent with summer, and not enough to fix the date.
  • “Two years ago, having served my time” Hatherley's career is consistent with an 1889 case.
  • “It appeared in all the papers about a year ago.” The earlier Hayling disappearance was about a year before, i.e. summer 1888.
  • “I had returned to civil practice and had finally abandoned Holmes in his Baker Street rooms” Watson is married and in separate practice.

Contradictions in the text

  • “not long after my marriage” Watson is already married in SCAN (March 1888) and FIVE (September 1887), and married in BOSC and TWIS (June 1889), so 'not long after' is hard to reconcile with a marriage dated before or around SIGN.

The Cardboard Box August 1889 · low confidence

A Friday in August (Parliament risen, a heatwave), after Watson has 'chronicled' SIGN; the first August after the SIGN case (September 1888) is August 1889, the next 1890. It sits oddly with the evidence that Watson was married by 1889, as he is at Baker Street with a depleted bank account. between 2 August 1889 and 29 August 1890

  • “the investigations which you have chronicled under the names of ‘A Study in Scarlet’ and of ‘The Sign of Four,’” SIGN is already chronicled; the SIGN case is September 1888, so the earliest August is August 1889.
  • “It was a blazing hot day in August.” Month and weather of day 1.
  • “To-day is Friday.” Day 1 is a Friday: August 1889 Fridays are the 2nd, 9th, 16th, 23rd and 30th; 1890: the 1st, 8th, 15th, 22nd and 29th. No day is fixed.
  • “Parliament had risen.” The summer recess has begun: mid to late August (real dates unverified: 1889 was prorogued late in August).
  • “In last year’s Anthropological Journal you will find two short monographs from my pen upon the subject.” Holmes's monographs on the ear date from the year before; no year is given.
  • “The box had been sent by parcel post from Belfast” Parcel post (introduced in Britain in 1883: unverified) places the story after 1883; consistent, not decisive.

Contradictions in the text
