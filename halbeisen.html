The Halbeisen Chronology

When each case takes place, from the Canon alone

Each date below is worked out from what the story itself says: stated dates, weekdays tested against the calendar, seasons, Watson’s marriage and lodgings, and references to other cases. No other chronologist was consulted. Cases are in the order we date them; 60 of 60 are done so far. A date marked [1] is the Canon’s own. Click a quoted passage to open the story at that place, with the passage marked.

The Gloria Scott 1876 · low confidence

About 1876 (Holmes's first visit to Donnithorpe in the long vacation, the Trevor tragedy in the following autumn), but the year can only be bracketed because the text contradicts itself: 'more than twenty years' after the 1855 voyage puts it no earlier than about 1876, while Holmes's college years must fall well before his London career, the Musgrave case and STUD (1881). Holmes tells it to Watson on a winter's night while they share rooms (after STUD, before Watson's marriage). between 1875 and 1880

  • “It was the year ’55 when the Crimean war was at its height” The convict voyage is 1855 (Falmouth 8 October, destruction 6 November); every later interval in the story is counted from here.
  • “For more than twenty years we have led peaceful and useful lives” Trevor's letter is written more than twenty years after the 1855-56 convict years (and the peaceful years began only after the diggings and travels), so the case is no earlier than about 1876.
  • “He was the only friend I made during the two years I was at college.” Holmes's college years are two; the visit to Donnithorpe falls in one of them. Holmes's age is not given in this story.
  • “All this occurred during the first month of the long vacation.” Hudson's visit is in the summer vacation (about July); the case starts with that visit.
  • “when the autumn was far advanced and the vacation drawing to a close” The recall to Donnithorpe, Trevor's death and the reading of the papers fall in October or thereabouts, the same year, so the whole case is about three to four months.
  • “Because it was the first in which I was ever engaged.” Holmes's first case, in his college days: before the Musgrave case and before he knew Watson (STUD, 1881), so the case cannot be later than about 1880.
  • “as we sat one winter’s night on either side of the fire” The telling: a winter's night with Watson sharing rooms at Baker Street, so between the winter of 1881-82 and the winter of 1887-88 (after STUD, before Watson's marriage).
  • “if they are of any use to your collection” Watson is already collecting Holmes's cases when he is told this, consistent with a telling after STUD.

Contradictions in the text

  • “Why, it’s thirty year and more since I saw you last.” Hudson counts thirty-plus years from 1855, i.e. 1885 or later, which is impossible for a case from Holmes's college days before he met Watson in 1881. The two 'thirty years' figures (this and Trevor's letter) agree with each other but contradict the story's own structure, so they are treated as the slip; 'more than twenty years' is the nearest figure that can be fitted.
  • “the laws were more harshly administered thirty years ago than now” Second 'thirty years' figure (letter written 1885 or later); same objection as Hudson's remark.
  • “who was a fine, robust old man” Armitage was 23 in 1855, so born about 1832; he would be only about 44 in 1876, hardly an 'old man' in the 'closing years of my life'. This points to a later date and agrees with the thirty-year figures, not with the college-days setting.

Order against other cases

  • “Because it was the first in which I was ever engaged.” before STUD: Holmes's very first case, in his college days, so before the Musgrave case and before STUD (March 1881).
  • “Because it was the first in which I was ever engaged.” before MUSG: MUSG calls itself Holmes's third case after settling in London, and refers back to this one as what first turned him to his profession.

The Musgrave Ritual 1880 · low confidence

Probably about 1880, shortly before Holmes met Watson (STUD): it is the third case of his London career, brought by a fellow-student who last saw him four years earlier, while Holmes still had rooms in Montague Street. No year or season is stated; Holmes tells it on a winter's night while Watson shares his rooms (after STUD, before Watson's marriage). between 1877 and 1880

  • “The third of these cases was that of the Musgrave Ritual” Holmes's third case after coming up to London; it comes after the Gloria Scott (his first) and after he had left the university.
  • “For four years I had seen nothing of him until one morning he walked into my room in Montague Street.” Musgrave calls four years after the two last met at college, so at least four years after Holmes's student days.
  • “When I first came up to London I had rooms in Montague Street” Holmes is still in Montague Street, i.e. before the move to Baker Street that preceded STUD.
  • “Even when you knew me first, at the time of the affair which you have commemorated in ‘A Study in Scarlet,’ I had already established a considerable, though not a very lucrative, connection.” The case precedes STUD (1881): it is one of the early cases kept in the tin box from before Watson.
  • “You may remember how the affair of the Gloria Scott” MUSG is later than GLOR, itself a college-days case, which Holmes had already told Watson.
  • “One winter’s night, as we sat together by the fire” The telling is on a winter's night, with Watson a fellow-lodger; the case itself has no stated season.
  • “I had heard him mention the case more than once, though I had never been able to gather the details.” Watson has lived with Holmes for some time before the telling, so the telling is not in the first weeks of 1881.
  • “One day last week—on Thursday night, to be more exact” The case begins about a week after a Thursday; no date can be derived from it.

Contradictions in the text

  • “It was nine feet in length.” A six-foot rod throwing a nine-foot shadow means the sun stands about 34 degrees high (my own calculation), yet Holmes has just called the sun 'low in the heavens' and the setting sun lights the passage minutes later. The sun therefore gives no reliable season.
  • “how long I had to wait before I succeeded in making any headway” Holmes stresses a long wait in London, but four years since leaving college plus two earlier cases leave little room before Watson meets him in 1881 if the Gloria Scott dates from about 1876; the chain is tight, which is why the date is put at the late end of the possible range.

Order against other cases

  • “You may remember how the affair of the Gloria Scott” after GLOR: Holmes cites the Gloria Scott as what first turned him to his profession; MUSG is a later case.
  • “Even when you knew me first, at the time of the affair which you have commemorated in ‘A Study in Scarlet,’ I had already established a considerable, though not a very lucrative, connection.” before STUD: Holmes dates the case from before he knew Watson.

A Study in Scarlet 4 March 1881 · medium confidence

The case begins on 4 March 1881: Watson gives the day and month but never the year, which follows from his nine months' sick leave after Maiwand and Jefferson Hope's 'twenty years' since 1860. Watson first met Holmes some weeks earlier, about January 1881 (no later than early February), and moved into Baker Street the next day. between 4 March 1881 and 4 March 1882

  • “It was upon the 4th of March, as I have good reason to remember” Day and month of the summons to Lauriston Gardens and of Holmes's revelation of his trade, the first morning of the Drebber case. No year is given anywhere in the story.
  • “with permission from a paternal government to spend the next nine months in attempting to improve it” Nine months' leave counted from landing at Portsmouth. Watson was wounded at Maiwand, then 'for months' ill with enteric fever, then a month at sea, so landing is late in 1880 at the earliest (Maiwand was fought on 27 July 1880: unverified, the text gives no date). The following 4 March is therefore March 1881; by March 1882 the leave would have run out.
  • “That girl that I spoke of was to have married me twenty years ago.” Hope's own reckoning. With the grave-board 'Died August 4th, 1860' (John Ferrier) the murders fall about 1880-81; 'twenty long years of longing' is a round figure, so 1881 is the nearest fit and 1879 or 1884 are far out.
  • “As the weeks went by, my interest in him and my curiosity as to his aims in life, gradually deepened and increased.” Watson had been lodging with Holmes for some weeks before 4 March, so the meeting at the hospital laboratory is well before it.
  • “During the first week or so we had no callers” After a first quiet week callers follow (Lestrade 'three or four times in a single week', a young girl, others) over a further stretch of time. The move to Baker Street is therefore at least three to five weeks before 4 March, i.e. no later than early February 1881.
  • “That very evening I moved my things round from the hotel” Watson inspected the rooms the day after Stamford introduced him to Holmes (Chapter II opens 'We met next day'), and moved in that evening. Landing in late 1880 and then living 'for some time' in a Strand hotel puts the meeting about January 1881.
  • “The Standard commented upon the fact that lawless outrages of the sort usually occurred under a Liberal Administration.” A Liberal government is in office; Gladstone's ministry ran from April 1880 to June 1885 (unverified), which agrees with 1881 and not with 1879.
  • “and this is the slack season” The landlady's remark on the lodgers' high rent fits March, an off-season month for London boarding-houses. Season only, no year.

Contradictions in the text

  • “The two bade adieu to their landlady upon Tuesday, the 4th inst., and departed to Euston Station” The Standard gives 'Tuesday, the 4th' for the departure from Camberwell. Watson dates the next morning 4 March and Lestrade puts the Euston sighting on 'the evening of the third'; 4 March was a Tuesday only in 1879, 1884 and 1890, years the Afghan war and the nine months' leave exclude (it was a Friday in 1881). One newspaper item against two other statements: the paper's weekday and date are the slip.
  • “They had been seen together at Euston Station about half-past eight on the evening of the third.” Lestrade's 'the third' (and the body found 'at two in the morning', so the 4th) disagrees with the Standard's 'Tuesday, the 4th inst.' for the departure; the evening of 3 March is taken as correct.
  • “In three years he was better off than his neighbours, in six he was well-to-do, in nine he was rich, and in twelve there were not half a dozen men” The Utah flashback counts twelve years from May 1847 to the June meeting with Lucy (1859), yet Ferrier's grave-board reads 'Died August 4th, 1860', a month after that meeting. The only exact date (1860) agrees with 'twenty years ago' and 1881; the 'twelve' is the loose figure.

The Resident Patient October 1881 · low confidence

October 1881 on Watson's own reckoning ('towards the end of the first year' in Baker Street, windy October, Watson still convalescent and without a practice), but he admits he is unsure of the date and Holmes's gang story (a bank robbery in 1875, fifteen-year sentences, release 'some years before' the full term) reads more naturally as the mid-1880s. between 1 October 1881 and 31 October 1887

  • “it must have been towards the end of the first year during which Holmes and I shared chambers in Baker Street” Watson's own dating: the first year of sharing Baker Street (begun about January 1881 per STUD), so the October of 1881.
  • “It was boisterous October weather” Month: October; windy day, clear evening, and Holmes later 'half-past seven next morning, in the first glimmer of daylight'.
  • “I because I feared with my shaken health to face the keen autumn wind” Watson is still an invalid from Afghanistan, supporting an early date soon after STUD.
  • “A retired Army surgeon.” Watson describes himself without any civil practice: before he has a practice, i.e. well before his marriage.
  • “I cannot be sure of the exact date, for some of my memoranda upon the matter have been mislaid” Watson admits his date is an inference, not a record.
  • “This was in 1875.” The Worthingdon bank robbery, from which the later dates are counted.
  • “When they got out the other day, which was some years before their full term” Fifteen years from 1875 ends in 1890; release 'some years' early points to about 1883-88, later than 1881.
  • “during the last few years I have made him a rich man” Trevelyan has been in practice for several years, which does not rule out 1881 but fits a later date better.

Contradictions in the text

  • “When they got out the other day, which was some years before their full term” If the case is October 1881 the three men were freed after about six of their fifteen years, nine years early; 'some years before' more naturally means three or four, i.e. about 1886-88 (the normal remission is unverified). This conflicts with Watson's 'first year' statement; Watson's own date is kept, but it is uncertain and he says so.
  • “it must have been towards the end of the first year during which Holmes and I shared chambers in Baker Street” Strictly, the first year ran from about January-February 1881 to the same time in 1882, so October would be nine months in, not 'towards the end'.

Order against other cases

  • “it must have been towards the end of the first year during which Holmes and I shared chambers in Baker Street” after STUD: The case lies in the first year of the Baker Street lodging, later than the March 1881 case of STUD.

The Speckled Band April 1883[1] · high confidence

Early April 1883, stated by Watson, while he still shared rooms with Holmes. Helen Stoner's account (sister died two years ago, engagement at Christmas two years ago, her own engagement a month before) is consistent. between 1 April 1883 and 10 April 1883

  • “It was early in April in the year ’83 that I woke one morning to find Sherlock Holmes standing, fully dressed, by the side of my bed” Explicit month and year.
  • “when we were sharing rooms as bachelors in Baker Street” Watson is unmarried and lodging at 221B, so before his marriage.
  • “Julia went there at Christmas two years ago” Counting back from April 1883 gives Christmas 1880; consistent.
  • “She died just two years ago, and it is of her death that I wish to speak to you.” Julia's death was about April 1881.
  • “but in a month or six weeks I shall be married” Wedding due in May or early June 1883.
  • “It is a little cold for the time of the year” Cold early-April morning.
  • “from which I have only been freed during the last month by the untimely death of the lady to whom the pledge was given” Written some years later, after Helen's death.

Contradictions in the text

  • “I shall walk down to Doctors’ Commons” Wills were kept at Somerset House by 1883 (unverified), so this is a minor anachronism; it does not affect the date.

The Yellow Face March 1887 · low confidence

Early spring (about March), with Watson living at Baker Street; the year is not stated. A voluntary transfer of a wife's property points to a marriage after 1882 (unverified), and Watson's marriage after September 1888 closes the window, so 1886 to spring 1889, with 1887 a weak favourite. between 1 March 1886 and 30 April 1889

  • “One day in early spring he had so far relaxed as to go for a walk with me in the Park” Season of day 1 (a Saturday by the counting of days).
  • “the sticky spear-heads of the chestnuts were just beginning to burst into their five-fold leaves” Bud-burst: late March to mid April.
  • “we could see in the light of the station lamps that he was very pale” Lamps needed on a 7 p.m. arrival: March or early April, consistent with early spring.
  • “I am a married man, and have been so for three years.” Munro's marriage is three years old, fixing nothing alone but see the next item.
  • “When we married, my wife made over all her property to me” A voluntary transfer 'rather against my will' suggests a marriage after the Married Women's Property Act took effect on 1 January 1883 (unverified), so the case is no earlier than early 1886.
  • “my friend and I have listened to a good many strange secrets in this room” A long partnership: well after the move in 1881.
  • “Save for the occasional use of cocaine, he had no vices” Cocaine only occasional: unlike the habitual injections 'three times a day for many months' of SIGN (September 1888) and the heavy period before SCAN (March 1888), so the spring of 1888 is the least likely of the candidates.

Contradictions in the text

  • “Well, about six weeks ago she came to me.” Munro says six weeks, then 'Nearly two months.'; two figures for the same time, a minor slip.

The Reigate Squire 25 April 1887[1] · high confidence

Monday 25 April 1887, the night of the Cunningham murder: Watson dates Holmes's breakdown to 'the spring of ’87', the Lyons telegram to 14 April (a Thursday), the return to Baker Street three days after reaching Lyons (Monday 18th), and the arrival at Reigate 'a week after' (Monday 25th); Holmes's notice speaks of 'Tuesday morning'. between 24 April 1887 and 26 April 1887

  • “his immense exertions in the spring of ’87” The year is stated: 1887, spring.
  • “it was upon the 14th of April that I received a telegram from Lyons” 14 April 1887 was a Thursday (calendar check).
  • “Within twenty-four hours I was in his sick-room” Watson reaches Lyons by Friday 15 April.
  • “Three days later we were back in Baker Street together” Back in London about Monday 18 April.
  • “a week after our return from Lyons we were under the Colonel’s roof” Arrival at Reigate about Monday 25 April, the evening of the gun-room talk.
  • “It was quarter to twelve when the alarm broke out.” The murder is the night of arrival, 11.45 p.m., reported at breakfast the next morning (Tuesday 26 April).
  • “at about a quarter to one on Tuesday morning an attempt was made” Holmes's deliberately faulty notice names Tuesday as the day, so the murder night is Monday night into Tuesday, agreeing with the count from 14 April.
  • “had his house broken into last Monday” The Acton burglary was Monday 18 April, a week before the murder, as the Colonel says on the evening of arrival.
  • “the thought of a week of spring time in the country” Season: spring.

Contradictions in the text

  • “It was quarter to twelve when the alarm broke out.” The alarm was at 11.45 p.m. (Monday night) but Holmes's notice says a quarter to one on Tuesday morning; Holmes says he misstated the time on purpose, so 'Tuesday' is read as the day of the notice, not a contradiction. Read strictly, the murder would be after midnight and the date would move one day later (26 April).

Order against other cases

  • “his immense exertions in the spring of ’87” before FIVE: FIVE is set in the latter days of September 1887; REIG is spring 1887.

The Five Orange Pips September 1887[1] · high confidence

Late September 1887: Watson says so ('the latter days of September'; 'the year ’87') and Openshaw's own count (January 1885 plus two years and eight months) gives September 1887. The text's references to Watson's marriage and to the Sign of the Four sit badly with 1887. between 20 September 1887 and 30 September 1887

  • “It was in the latter days of September, and the equinoctial gales had set in with exceptional violence.” Time of year: after the equinox, late September.
  • “It was in January, ’85, that my poor father met his end, and two years and eight months have elapsed since then.” January 1885 plus 2 years 8 months is September 1887.
  • “The year ’87 furnished us with a long series of cases of greater or less interest” Watson places the case in 1887.
  • “One day—it was in March, 1883—a letter with a foreign stamp lay upon the table” Earlier family dates (10 March and 2 May 1883) are consistent with the 1885 and 1887 counts.
  • “My wife was on a visit to her mother’s, and for a few days I was a dweller once more in my old quarters at Baker Street.” Watson is already married in September 1887.
  • “Very long and very severe were the equinoctial gales that year.” Confirms the autumn gales of that year.

Contradictions in the text

  • “My wife was on a visit to her mother’s” Watson is married in September 1887 here, yet NOBL (October 1887) says his marriage is still weeks away, and SIGN, which this story cites, has dating pointing to 1888.
  • ““Save, perhaps, the Sign of Four.”” Treats SIGN as already past in September 1887; SIGN's own text (Dec 1878 'nearly ten years ago') points to about September 1888.
  • “I have been beaten four times—three times by men, and once by a woman.” If the woman is Irene Adler (SCAN, 20 March 1888) she is in the future in September 1887; the identification is a guess.
  • ““It is really two days since you had the letter.” Openshaw said the letter came 'yesterday morning', so one day, not two.

The Noble Bachelor 6 October 1887 · medium confidence

Early October 1887: Lord St Simon, born in 1846, is 'forty-one', and the hotel bill is dated 4 October with the wedding on the Wednesday (5 October 1887), so Holmes is consulted a day or two later. It is set 'a few weeks before my own marriage', which conflicts with SCAN, FIVE and the date of SIGN. between 5 October 1887 and 8 October 1887

  • “Born in 1846.’ He’s forty-one years of age” Year 1887 by age arithmetic.
  • “‘Oct. 4th, rooms 8s., breakfast 2s. 6d., cocktail 1s., lunch 2s. 6d., glass sherry, 8d.’” Hotel bill dated 4 October, within a week of the case.
  • “Two days later—that is, on Wednesday last—there is a curt announcement that the wedding had taken place” Wedding on a Wednesday; 5 October 1887 was a Wednesday.
  • “for the weather had taken a sudden turn to rain, with high autumnal winds” Autumn.
  • “It was a few weeks before my own marriage, during the days when I was still sharing rooms with Holmes in Baker Street” Set before Watson's marriage.
  • “how to while away these bleak autumnal evenings” Closing line confirms autumn.
  • “Frank here and I met in ’84, in McQuire’s camp, near the Rockies” Hatty's history fits a wedding about three years later, 1887.

Contradictions in the text

  • “It was a few weeks before my own marriage” Watson is married by March 1888 (SCAN) and already in September 1887 (FIVE); if his marriage follows SIGN (about September 1888) this story would be October 1888, but St Simon's age says 1887.
  • ““My last client of the sort was a king.”” Holmes's royal client in SCAN was the King of Bohemia (March 1888), called here 'the King of Scandinavia'; if the same, this story is after March 1888, contradicting 1887.

The Valley of Fear 7 January 1888 · low confidence

About 7 January 1888 (the text gives the day and month, the murder being the night of 6 January): Watson places it 'at the end of the ’80’s', Moriarty is still alive and Watson still lodges at Baker Street, and Barker's figures (Douglas in California five years, left six or seven years ago) fit a start soon after the 1875 trial. The Vermissa flashback is 1875 and its 'twenty years' and 'ten years' figures contradict this. between 7 January 1887 and 7 January 1889

  • “Those were the early days at the end of the ’80’s, when Alec MacDonald was far from having attained the national fame which he has now achieved.” Watson's own dating of the Birlstone case: the late 1880s.
  • “Being the seventh of January, we have very properly laid in the new almanac.” The case opens on 7 January; the murder was on the night of 6 January.
  • “And then you were together five years in California, so that this business dates back not less than eleven years at the least?” Douglas left California 'six years ago, nearer seven' after five years there, so he arrived there 11 or 12 years before the case; with 1875-76 as the end of the Vermissa events this gives about 1887-88.
  • “He has been with Douglas ever since he took the Manor House five years ago.” Douglas settled at Birlstone five years earlier, about 1883; consistent with a 1888 case and a California departure about 1881.
  • “I can tell a Moriarty when I see one.” Moriarty is alive and active, so the case is before May 1891.
  • “Late last night Mrs. Hudson, our landlady, brought up a message” Watson shares Baker Street and writes 'our landlady': before his marriage, which follows SIGN (September 1888).
  • “when the time comes we’ll all hope for a place in your book” Watson is already known as Holmes's chronicler, so after STUD was written (1887).
  • “It was the fourth of February in the year 1875.” Anchor of the Vermissa flashback, set well before the Birlstone case; not the date of the Birlstone case.

Contradictions in the text

  • “I wish you to journey back some twenty years in time” Twenty years before 1875 would put Birlstone about 1895; but Moriarty alive, Watson unmarried at Baker Street and 'the end of the ’80’s' all fix the late 1880s. 'Twenty years' is the slip (only 12 or 13 years from 1875 to 1888).
  • “For ten years they were out of the world, and then came a day when they were free once more” If the Scowrers served ten years from 1875-76 they were freed about 1885-86, yet Barker says men were already asking after Douglas in California about 1881 (six or seven years before the case); the two cannot both hold.
  • “In age he may have been about fifty” McMurdo is 'not far from his thirtieth year' in 1875, so Douglas would be about 43 in 1888, about 50 only about 1895.
  • “You have heard me speak of Professor Moriarty?” Watson has already heard of Moriarty, but in FINA (April 1891) Watson 'never' heard of him; also Holmes here has been three times to Moriarty's rooms yet FINA treats their first meeting as the Baker Street call.

Order against other cases

  • “I don’t say that he can’t be beat. But you must give me time—you must give me time!” before FINA: Moriarty is alive and Holmes has not yet beaten him; the Reichenbach struggle of May 1891 (FINA) follows.

A Scandal in Bohemia 20 March 1888[1] · high confidence

The story opens on the evening of 20 March 1888, a date Watson states outright; the case runs over that evening and the next day. The text's own weekday arithmetic (betrothal on 'next Monday', 'three days yet') does not fit 20 March, which was a Tuesday. between 16 March 1888 and 23 March 1888

  • “One night—it was on the twentieth of March, 1888—I was returning from a journey to a patient” Explicit date of the first evening, and of the King's visit at a quarter to eight that night.
  • “I had seen little of Holmes lately. My marriage had drifted us away from each other.” Watson is already married and living apart, so the story is after his marriage.
  • “If you will be good enough to call to-morrow afternoon at three o’clock” The case runs over a second day, 21 March; the wedding and the departure of Adler follow on that day.
  • “It was already dusk, and the lamps were just being lighted” Dusk at about ten to seven fits late March (around the equinox); the fire and the ulster fit the cool season.
  • “which must always be associated in my mind with my wooing” The Sign of the Four, with Watson's wooing, is already past, so SIGN precedes March 1888 on this text.
  • “by binding you both to absolute secrecy for two years; at the end of that time the matter will be of no importance” Watson may publish only after two years, consistent with writing in or after 1890 (published 1891).

Contradictions in the text

  • “betrothal was publicly proclaimed. That will be next Monday.” A Monday follows 'three days yet' (next quote) only if the first evening is a Friday (16 or 23 March 1888). 20 March 1888 was a Tuesday, so the next Monday is six days away.
  • ““Oh, then we have three days yet,” said Holmes with a yawn.” Contradicts the stated 20 March (a Tuesday); one of the two statements is a slip.
  • “‘Come, man, come, only three minutes, or it won’t be legal.’” A noon deadline for a church wedding fits the law before 1886 (unverified), which sits oddly with 1888.
  • “which must always be associated in my mind with my wooing” SIGN's own text (Mary Morstan's father vanished 3 December 1878, 'nearly ten years ago'; a September evening) points to about September 1888, after this story; Watson's marriage cannot both precede March 1888 and follow SIGN.

The Greek Interpreter August 1888 · low confidence

A summer (probably late summer) evening, most likely in 1888: Watson is still living with Holmes at Baker Street, and Mycroft greets him as Holmes's 'chronicler', which presupposes that STUD (published late 1887) has made him known. The case runs from a Monday night to the Wednesday evening. between 1 June 1888 and 22 September 1888

  • “It was after tea on a summer evening” Season: summer.
  • “I hear of Sherlock everywhere since you became his chronicler.” Watson's accounts are already known, so after STUD became public (published November-December 1887, unverified for this purpose): the first such summer is 1888.
  • “We had reached our house in Baker Street while we had been talking.” Watson speaks of 'our house' and 'our room': he is still living at Baker Street, i.e. unmarried; his marriage follows SIGN (September 1888).
  • “During my long and intimate acquaintance with Mr. Sherlock Holmes” Several years since January 1881, fitting 1888 better than the early 1880s.
  • “It was almost dark before we found ourselves in Pall Mall” The party reaches Pall Mall by about 8 p.m. (they must be at London Bridge by 9.45 after more than an hour at Scotland Yard); 'almost dark' then suggests sunset before about 7.45 p.m., i.e. mid-August or later rather than June or July (unverified astronomy).
  • “It was a quarter to ten before we reached London Bridge” Timetable used for the dusk argument above.
  • “This is Wednesday evening,” said Mr. Melas.” The telling is on a Wednesday, two days after the Monday-night abduction; no month or year.
  • “It’s six now, so if you care for a stroll this beautiful evening” Fine summer weather at six o'clock when the Diogenes Club visit begins.

Order against other cases

  • “I hear of Sherlock everywhere since you became his chronicler.” after STUD: Watson's chronicling (STUD) has already made Holmes talked about.
  • “We had reached our house in Baker Street while we had been talking.” before CROO: Watson still lives at Baker Street here; by CROO he is married with his own house.

A Case of Identity September 1888 · low confidence

After March 1888 (it cites the Irene Adler case) and with Holmes still alive, the only Fridays the 14th that fit are 14 Sept 1888, 14 Dec 1888, 14 June 1889, and 14 Feb, 14 Mar or 14 Nov 1890. No year is stated; September 1888 is the weak favourite on weather (straw hat with fur boa) and the 'Hague last year' link. between 14 September 1888 and 14 November 1890

  • “on the morning of the fourteenth, a gentleman named Hosmer Angel” The wedding morning was the 14th of a month.
  • “Your wedding was arranged, then, for the Friday.” The 14th was a Friday, which restricts the month and year.
  • “I advertised for him in last Saturday’s Chronicle” Ad on Saturday the 15th; the consultation falls in the following week (a few days after the 14th).
  • “It is a little souvenir from the King of Bohemia in return for my assistance in the case of the Irene Adler papers.” After SCAN (20 March 1888), so not before March 1888.
  • “I forgot that I had not seen you for some weeks.” Watson is living apart and married; supports the married period.
  • “there was something of the sort at The Hague last year” A Hague case 'last year'; possibly linked to the Dutch royal commission done before March 1888 (unverified link), which would favour 1888.
  • “a slate-coloured, broad-brimmed straw hat” A straw hat with a fur boa and muff suggests a spring or autumn day, not deep winter or high summer.

Contradictions in the text
