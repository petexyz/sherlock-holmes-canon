Sherlock Holmes Canon: Historical Chronology

When each case takes place, as opposed to when it was published. Doyle left few firm dates, so every column is one reading of the clues. Click a heading to sort by it. Halbeisen: this project’s own reading, dated from the Canon alone (60 of 60 so far); click a date for the reasoning. Baring-Gould: William S. Baring-Gould, whose dates (The Chronological Holmes, 1955; used again in The Annotated Sherlock Holmes, 1967) are the best-known standard. Bell: H. W. Bell, Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson: The Chronology of Their Adventures, the first full chronology. Christ: Jay Finley Christ, An Irregular Chronology. Brend: Gavin Brend, My Dear Holmes. Zeisler: Ernest Bloomfield Zeisler, Baker Street Chronology. Keefauver: Brad Keefauver’s online chronology. Crauser: Jean-Pierre Crauser, a French chronology. Miller: Chris J. Miller’s chronology (undated; our source gives no year), the most precise of the three lists we began with: a month or day for most cases. “?” marks his own doubt. Klinger: Leslie S. Klinger’s table: the year most of fifteen chronologists agree on (Peck and Klinger, “The Date Being—?”, 1996). “*” means they did not agree and the year is the editor’s choice. Toronto: the Bootmakers of Toronto’s reading list (an undated web page that follows Klinger), year only. Dates for the published chronologists are taken from transcriptions on the web (mainly the Arthur Conan Doyle Encyclopedia), not checked against the books.
